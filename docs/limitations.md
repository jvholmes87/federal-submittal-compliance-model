# Limitations

## Scope

The instrument is deployed and in use by a small subcontractor performing federal construction task orders at an overseas installation. The available observations come from one organization and one operating context. The artifact documents architecture and preliminary measurements rather than a general estimate of federal-construction performance.

The current work is appropriately framed as a conference-paper-scale study. It is not yet doctoral in scope because no generalizable theoretical, measurement, or comparative contribution has been established.

## Scholarly gap not yet established

The practical problem is real in the studied setting, but the scholarly novelty of the proposed contribution has **not** been established. Established construction-management and engineering-management literature may already explain parts of the observed problem through research on rework causation, RFIs, submittal review, defect taxonomies, requirements change, process delay, and administrative burden.

A focused literature review therefore precedes further codebook or taxonomy development. The repository must not describe the problem as unexplored, novel, or dissertation-ready until that review establishes what is already known and what remains unresolved.

## Single-organization sample

The sample reflects one organization's contracts, personnel structure, prime-contractor routing, government interfaces, and record practices. These conditions may differ across agencies, installations, contract vehicles, primes, and firms. The current data cannot separate organization-specific effects from wider process effects.

## No control group

No comparable organization or project group operates without the instrument under otherwise matched conditions. Changes over time could reflect project mix, reviewer behavior, workforce learning, contract requirements, or other causes. The current observations cannot support a causal claim that the instrument changed approval or timeliness outcomes.

## No inter-rater reliability estimate

Gate applicability, gate type, state, evidence sufficiency, and rejection categories have not yet been independently coded by multiple raters. No inter-rater reliability statistic has been computed. Classification consistency therefore remains an unmeasured source of error.

## First-pass metric definitions

The 11 September 2026 research snapshot uses two first-pass submittal measures that must remain separate:

- **Line-item first-pass yield:** 20 first-pass approvals among 29 dispositioned line items = 69 percent after rounding.
- **Package first-pass yield:** 8 first-pass approvals among 17 packages = 47 percent after rounding.

These units answer different questions. A package may contain multiple line items, so package-level and line-item-level rates cannot be substituted for one another.

The dashboard's **85 percent current-state gate approval share** is also a different metric. It describes the present distribution of gate states, not the first formal disposition of a submittal.

A 3 September 2026 snapshot reported **43 percent (3 of 7)** using an earlier gate-row adjudication definition. That value remains useful only as a dated measurement-history observation. It is not the current first-pass submittal metric and must not be compared mechanically with the line-item or package rates.

An earlier **17 percent** figure is superseded. It mixed administrative gates with true adjudicated submittal rows and is retained only as the record of a measurement-design error. The correction is a metric-validity finding, not evidence that performance improved from 17 percent to any later percentage.

Every future rate must include the numerator, denominator, unit of analysis, eligibility rule, and snapshot date.

## Government-review latency

At the 11 September 2026 snapshot, 11 tracked items had been pending more than 14 days and the oldest had been pending 66 days, against a contractual one-work-week response obligation in the studied context.

These are portfolio observations, not a government-wide service-level estimate. The data do not by themselves establish the reason for delay, whether each elapsed interval is wholly government-controlled, or whether all items are directly comparable. Formal analysis must preserve submission date, response date, actor/owner, contractual timing rule, and any intervening dependency.

## Measurement effects

Deployment may change how obligations are identified, classified, and retained. Better detection can initially increase the recorded number of late, rejected, or incomplete items. Apparent deterioration may reflect improved visibility rather than a change in underlying performance.

The template also evolved during deployment. The current baseline has 61 gates, while project instances range from 60 to 72 rows. Analyses must distinguish template growth, applicable obligations, completed gates, adjudicated submittals, line items, and packages.

## Structural-conflict observation

The award-plus-30-day and access-dependency case demonstrates a logically infeasible sequence when access occurs after the deadline. It does not establish how often the condition occurs across projects or agencies. Access duration, personnel category, measurement requirements, and available contractual remedies require separate coding before prevalence can be estimated.

The stronger research value of the observation is the dependency structure itself: an obligation can be contractually due before an externally controlled prerequisite makes performance physically possible. That pattern requires validation across additional cases before it can support a general claim.

## Generalization requirements

Broader claims would require:

- Completion of the literature review and an explicit gap statement grounded in prior research
- A defensible unit of analysis and stable measurement definitions
- Multiple small contractors and subcontractors across different primes, agencies, locations, and contract types
- A preregistered definition of adjudication, first-pass approval, timeliness, compliance failure, and review latency
- Independent coding and an inter-rater reliability assessment where classification claims are made
- A stable observation period with consistent denominator rules
- Project-level covariates for scope, complexity, reviewer, contract type, and access dependency
- A comparison design, such as matched cohorts, phased implementation, or interrupted time series, for causal claims
- Sensitivity analysis for template changes, missing evidence, and state-classification choices
- Documented authority and consent for any operational data used beyond sanitized aggregates

Until those conditions are met, the repository should be read as a deployed architecture description and a record of preliminary, bounded measurements.
