# Research Roadmap

## Status

This roadmap governs the transition from a deployed single-organization research artifact to stronger conference and doctoral research. It does not claim that a doctoral gap has been established.

## Priority 1 — establish whether the research gap is real

Before expanding the rejection codebook, refining a taxonomy, or claiming novelty, conduct a focused literature review of established construction-management, engineering-management, and information-systems research.

### Target journals

- *Journal of Construction Engineering and Management*
- *Automation in Construction*
- *Journal of Management in Engineering*

### Search families

- rework causation
- submittal review
- RFI latency
- construction defect taxonomy
- requirements volatility
- regulatory compliance cost small firms

The review should also follow citation networks around major rework and construction-process researchers, including Peter E. D. Love and related authors, without assuming in advance that their work resolves the proposed question.

### Required output

The review must produce:

1. A search log and inclusion/exclusion record.
2. A literature evidence table.
3. A map of established constructs, measures, and causal explanations.
4. A clear statement of what is already known.
5. A bounded gap statement, or a finding that no doctoral gap has yet been established.

## Priority 2 — resolve the unit of analysis

After the literature review, define the analytical unit or hierarchy explicitly. Candidate units include line item, package, gate/obligation, correspondence thread, project, and organization. Different measures may require different units, but every reported rate must preserve its numerator, denominator, eligibility rule, and snapshot date.

## Priority 3 — develop the codebook and taxonomy

Only after Priorities 1 and 2 should the rejection/failure taxonomy be finalized. Established field terminology takes precedence over internal labels when an accepted construct already exists.

Any claim that the codebook is a validated instrument requires an explicit validation design, including independent coding where inter-rater reliability is claimed.

## Priority 4 — determine doctoral scope

The present single-organization measurement plus deployed tool is conference-paper scale. A doctoral contribution would need to generalize beyond this case. Three candidate paths are currently retained:

1. **Theoretical model:** explain why obligation burden falls disproportionately on small firms and identify testable mechanisms.
2. **Validated measurement instrument:** develop and validate an obligation-burden instrument that other researchers and organizations can apply.
3. **Comparative evidence:** test whether obligations enforced in a data model outperform obligations enforced primarily through procedure, using a defensible comparison or phased design.

Selection among these paths should follow the literature, advisor fit, feasible data access, and a defensible research design rather than a preferred degree label.

## Current publication boundary

The immediate research objective is a sanitized conference paper suitable for acquisition/systems-engineering review. The repository may document architecture and bounded empirical observations, but it must not claim causality, general prevalence, or doctoral novelty until the required evidence exists.
